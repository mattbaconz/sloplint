## Layout: Ledger

- First screen: evidence set as figures and ruled rows in the mono face, with the headline at `--step-4` or larger. Three forms that fit: (1) a before-and-after pair of figures side by side, with the difference stated in words; (2) a numbered index of the page's sections at display size, each with one concrete fact; (3) a short ruled table of three to five real line items, set large, under the headline. Pick one, or another that keeps the rule, and fill it from this brief's own material (what it sells or shows, its own words, names, places, dates and numbers), so it could not open any other page. It must hold at 1280px and at 375px, and the page should be recognizable from it as a thumbnail. A heading, a paragraph and two buttons on an empty background is the generic answer; do not ship it.
- Sections are numbered rows separated by hairline rules: the claim on the left, the evidence on the right.
- Figures sit in the mono face, right-aligned, so they read as a column.
- Signature: Hairline rules (1px `--line`) between sections and rows instead of boxed cards.
- Signature: Text-only navigation; the current item gets a 2px `--accent` underline.
- Grid: 12 columns, 24px gutters, content max 1200px. Sections 88px apart, 56px under 640px.
- At 375px nothing scrolls sideways: below 768px grids become one column (`grid-template-columns: minmax(0, 1fr)`; spanning all 12 still leaves 11 gutters) and navigation wraps or collapses.
- Tables at narrow widths: below 600px a table of more than four columns either stacks each row into a card (`display: block` on `tr` and `td`, the column name as a small mono label) or scrolls inside a `position: relative; overflow-x: auto` wrapper with a visible edge (a `--border` rule or the next column cut off), so the reader can tell it scrolls. Without `position: relative`, visually hidden labels inside the table escape the wrapper and widen the page.
